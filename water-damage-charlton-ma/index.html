Uplands, snow and the ice dam season

Charlton covers about 42 square miles, roughly 5 miles northeast of downtown Southbridge, and the land climbs from about 600 feet in the southeastern river valleys to over 1,000 feet in the north-central uplands. Dresser Hill on Route 31 is known for its long views. Hilltop houses catch wind, snow and freezing rain that valley homes shrug off.

That matters at the roofline. Heat escaping through an attic melts snow, the meltwater refreezes at the eaves and forms an ice dam, and the backed-up water seeps under shingles into ceilings and wall tops. Dispatch runs 24/7 around the clock, which covers the January night when a ceiling starts to drip.

For a ceiling already wet, water mitigation begins with moisture mapping and drying before the insulation holds the damp.

Basements, well houses and septic: what goes wrong

Charlton had no town-owned water supply historically. The 2017 town plan counted about 300 homes and businesses on public water, with everyone else on private wells, so most properties here carry their own water and wastewater equipment.

  • Basement seepage: Spring melt and a high water table push moisture through foundation cracks. The EPA advises drying water-damaged areas within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth.
  • Well house moisture: A damp well house can short the pump and leave damp insulation behind. Shut off the power to the pump before anyone enters.
  • Septic backup: Aging septic systems are named as a water-quality threat in the town plan. If drains gurgle or sewage surfaces, stop using water and keep out, because this is Category 3 water.
  • Condensation and hidden damp: Cold basements and unheated rooms collect moisture on walls and framing, which feeds mold when the source is not fixed.

When moisture has already fed mold on drywall or framing, mold remediation contains and removes it to the S520 standard. Fix the water first, and ask any company which Massachusetts requirements apply to the job.

Before we arrive

Four steps for a Charlton home, with its wells, septic tanks and hillside basements in mind.

Close the valve

Shut the main valve. On a well, turn off the pump breaker as well so it stops refilling the lines.

Cut power if dry

Switch off breakers to wet rooms only if the panel is dry and reachable. A wet well house or a flooded panel is a call to the utility first.

Lift valuables

Raise boxes, papers and electronics off the floor. Pull wet rugs back from hardwood so the finish does not stain.

Protect people and pets

If a septic tank or drain overflowed, keep everyone out of that area and away from the well.

Villages, roads and the Southbridge link

Charlton Center, Charlton City and Charlton Depot lie along Route 31, with Route 20 running east and west. The Mass Pike crosses town, but it has no exit in Charlton. Route 169 is the link to Southbridge, so tell dispatch which village and road you are near.

Per the plan, about 4,800 housing units stand in town, 81 percent owner-occupied, about 43 percent built before 1980 and 7 percent before 1940. Most owners handle claims and repairs themselves. Southbridge operates the public water system under a 2015 inter-municipal agreement while Charlton owns the pipes. The sewer plant discharges to Cady Brook, and the plan said it was being extended, so ask the town about your connection.

ZIP 01507 covers Charlton Center, Charlton City and Charlton Depot, and the top calls there are emergency water removal and mold remediation. Spencer, to the north, is close enough that callers sometimes ask about it. Neighbors in Dudley are served from the same line.
