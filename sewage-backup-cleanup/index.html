Emergency Sewage Backup Cleanup in Southbridge, MA

Sewage backup cleanup is the safe removal of wastewater and everything it soaked, followed by cleaning, sanitizing and drying of the space. The 24/7 line is open, so call once you are out of the affected area.

Why sewage is treated as Category 3 from the first minute

Wastewater from a toilet, a floor drain or a main line carries bacteria, viruses and solids. The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard classes it as Category 3, which it describes as grossly contaminated water. A published IICRC position statement adds that for Category 2 or 3 water, remediation should occur before restorative drying.

That order is the whole difference from a clean supply line leak. Nothing gets a fan pointed at it until the contaminated material is out and the surfaces are cleaned.

The drain is gurgling and the basement smells: what you are seeing

Most backups announce themselves before the floor is wet. Here is what each sign usually means.

  • Gurgling toilet when the washer drains: Air is being pushed past a partial blockage in the line you share with other fixtures.
  • Water rising in a basement floor drain or shower: The line is full below that point, and the lowest opening in the house is the first to give.
  • Several fixtures slow at once: The blockage is probably in the main house line or the street connection, not in one trap.
  • Sewer smell with no visible water: Liquid may already be under flooring or behind a baseboard.
  • Stained, damp carpet near a drain: Treat it as contaminated until a restorer says otherwise.

None of these proves the cause. They do tell you to stop adding water to the system.

First-hour safety: what to do and what to leave alone

Do not wade through it, and do not run the dishwasher, washer, shower or toilets. Every flush adds to the problem. Keep children, pets and anyone with a weak immune system out of the area, and wear rubber boots and gloves if you must cross it.

Cut power to the area only if the panel is dry and reachable without standing in water. If the water touches outlets or appliances, call the utility or 911 before the restoration line. The Massachusetts home care guide for sewage back-ups says to assume pathogens are present and take precautions.

Old pipes, heavy rain and the Southbridge sewer system

The town's 2024 draft hazard mitigation plan describes the sanitary sewer system as in generally poor condition, with significant inflow and infiltration during heavy precipitation. It also says the wastewater treatment plant reaches maximum treatment capacity multiple times a year. In plain terms, a heavy storm can load the lines that feed your house, so a backup is more likely on the wettest days.

A 2015 to 2016 EPA Region 1 stormwater report for the town says about 85 percent of the population is on sewer and 15 percent on septic. That split matters when you call: a municipal line problem is the town's to fix, while a septic overflow needs a septic pumper.

The same report lists 5,790 feet of sewer pipe root-treated and 16 short liners installed on cracked pipe in that year. Roots and cracks are everyday causes, and a private lateral from the house to the street is usually the owner's responsibility.

How a sewage cleanup runs, in order

Crews work in protective gear from the first step to the last.

  1. Assess and containA moisture meter and thermal imaging camera find how far the liquid traveled. Doors and vents are closed off so contaminated air stays put.
  2. ExtractTruck-mounted or portable extraction units pull the liquid out. Floor drains are checked so the source is not still feeding the room.
  3. Remove porous materialsCarpet, pad, low drywall, insulation and cardboard are cut out and bagged. The Massachusetts guide says upholstered furniture and mattresses should be discarded.
  4. Clean, then sanitizeThe state guide notes that cleaning and disinfection are two different processes. Hard surfaces are scrubbed first, then treated with an antimicrobial.
  5. Dry and verifyAir movers and dehumidifiers run until readings reach the dry standard. See structural drying for how that is monitored.

Does insurance cover a sewage backup?

It depends on how the water got in. The Massachusetts Division of Insurance says standard homeowners policies do not cover flood damage, and that some water backup or sump pump failure coverage may be available as an endorsement. Check your policy for that rider by name.

Flood policies are different. Federal flood coverage pays for backup through sewers and drains only when a flood in the area is the cause. Photograph everything before cleanup starts, keep damaged and undamaged items apart, and ask your insurer what it needs. Nothing here promises a claim will be paid.

Mistakes that make a backup worse

These come up often and are easy to avoid.

  • Mopping it up yourself with a household mop: It spreads contamination and leaves the mop as a hazard.
  • Pointing fans at it: Moving air carries contaminated particles into clean rooms.
  • Using undiluted bleach: The state guide says bleach should never be used directly without dilution, and it does not replace removal.
  • Keeping wet carpet and pad: The state guide lists carpet padding among items to discard.
  • Waiting to see if it drains down: Contact time is what drives the damage.
