How the service area works

Why each town gets its own page

A single template with a town name dropped in would be easy to write and no use to the person with water in the basement. The five town pages exist because the water problems differ. Sturbridge has hillside runoff and culverts that flood in heavy rain. Dudley sits between the French River and the Quinebaug River. Charlton is an upland town where many homes use private wells and septic systems.

Webster has a large lake, an old mill district and a river on its western edge. Oxford lies in the French River floodplain, below the Hodges Village Dam and a short distance downstream of the Buffumville Dam in Charlton. Those details change what a restoration crew checks first and what a homeowner should ask their insurer.

The pages draw on town websites, hazard mitigation plans prepared for the towns and state agency pages. Where a rule or number could not be confirmed, the page says to ask the town or check the FEMA flood map for your address instead of guessing.

For help choosing a service once you have found your town, the services overview matches common situations to the right page, and the line at (508) 446-9244 can sort out the order if more than one applies.

If your town is not listed

Brookfield, Holland and Spencer sit just beyond these five towns, and Thompson, Connecticut lies south of Dudley and Webster. They do not have pages of their own. If you are in one of them or somewhere else nearby, call (508) 446-9244 and ask whether the line can help at your address. The answer depends on availability on the day.

What every town page covers

The town pages are written separately, because a lakefront basement and a hilltop house with a private well do not have the same problems.

  • Local water sources: the rivers, lakes and brooks that flood in that town, taken from town and regional hazard mitigation plans.
  • Housing and utilities: how older buildings, private wells and septic systems change the work.
  • ZIP codes served: every ZIP for the town, tied to real roads and villages.
  • Emergency steps: what to do before help arrives, written for that town.
