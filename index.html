Why Southbridge is different

Older buildings, a river through downtown

What the housing stock means for water damage

Southbridge is an old mill town, and its buildings show it. The town's 2024 draft hazard mitigation plan reports that about 38.5 percent of occupied housing units were built before 1939 and about 81.6 percent were built in 1979 or earlier. Plan figures come from older Census estimates, so treat them as a guide to the building stock rather than an exact count.

In practical terms, that means plaster and lath walls, original cast iron and galvanized supply lines, and basements built long before modern moisture barriers. A slow leak inside an old wall can run for months before a stain appears, and drying a plaster ceiling is a different job from drying a modern drywall one.

The same plan counts multi-family homes at about 58.6 percent of occupied units, against 41.3 percent single-family. When a pipe fails on the third floor of a triple-decker, the water rarely stays in one unit, which is why a fast call matters for landlords and tenants alike.

The Quinebaug River and flood exposure

The whole town sits in the Quinebaug River Basin, and the river runs through downtown. The hazard plan puts about 6 percent of the town's land in the 100 or 500 year floodplain and lists 476 parcels as susceptible to a 100 year flood, 364 of them with structures.

Upstream, Westville Lake Dam is operated by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers and rated High Hazard in the plan. That rating describes the consequences if the dam were to fail, not a forecast that it will. It is still a reason to check your own address on the FEMA flood map and to ask your insurer about separate flood coverage.

Know before you call

Water categories and what they mean

Restoration crews classify water by how contaminated it is, using categories from the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard. The category decides what can be dried in place and what must be thrown away.

CategoryTypical sourceWhat it usually means
Category 1Broken supply line, overflowing sink with clean waterClean at the start. It can become Category 2 within about 48 hours if left standing.
Category 2Washing machine or dishwasher discharge, toilet overflow with urine onlySignificantly contaminated. Porous materials such as carpet pad are usually removed.
Category 3Sewage backup, river or storm floodingGrossly contaminated. Strict containment, removal of porous materials and sanitizing apply.

Ask any company to show how it classifies your water. A crew that skips the category question is not following the standard.

Documentation for your insurance claim

Sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe, is often covered by a homeowners policy, while gradual leaks and neglect often are not. Flood damage is usually excluded and needs separate flood coverage. Check your policy or ask your insurer what applies to you.

We document the loss with photos, moisture readings and a written scope of work you can hand to your adjuster. We do not promise a claim will be approved.
