Emergency Flood Damage Restoration in Southbridge, MA

Flood damage restoration is the removal of storm, river or groundwater from a building, followed by cleanup, sanitizing and drying of everything the water touched. The emergency line is open 24/7, so call as soon as it is safe to do so.

While you wait, stay out of rising water, keep people and pets away, lift valuables to a dry level, and shut off the main water valve if the water came from a pipe. Call the utility or 911 first if electrical or gas is a risk.

Stay out first: electrical and gas come before the restoration call

Floodwater and electricity are a deadly mix. If water is at or above outlets, or touching a panel, a furnace or a water heater, do not go in. Call the utility or 911 for any electrical or gas risk before you call the restoration line.

Cut power only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without standing in water. If you smell gas or hear hissing, leave the building and call from outside. Flood restoration begins after the area is safe to enter.

The first hours of a flood: why the emergency cannot wait

Flood water is classed as Category 3 under the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard because it carries soil, runoff and possibly sewage. Porous materials such as carpet, pad and insulation that sat in it are usually removed, not dried. Every hour that wet drywall stays in place, the water wicks higher up the wall.

Pumping and extraction come first, then removal of soaked material, then sanitizing and drying. Night, weekend and holiday calls are answered the same way. Same-day response is offered when available, and no arrival time is promised.

Basement Flooding Cleanup

Basements flood from rain that overwhelms the ground, a failed sump pump, a high water table or a river that spills its banks. Crews extract the water, cut out wet carpet, pad and low drywall, then dry the block and framing. Restoration repairs damage that has already happened and is not waterproofing. The step-by-step detail is on the basement water damage page.

Sewage Backup Cleanup

Sewage backup is Category 3 water from the first minute. Stay out of it, do not run water or flush toilets, and keep children and pets away. Crews work in protective gear, remove affected porous materials and clean and treat the hard surfaces. For safety rules, equipment and what changes the cost, see the sewage backup cleanup page.

The Quinebaug River and flood risk in Southbridge

The whole town is in the Quinebaug River Basin, and the river runs through downtown. The town's 2024 draft hazard mitigation plan puts about 6 percent of town land in the 100 or 500 year floodplain. It lists 476 parcels susceptible to a 100 year flood, 364 of them with structures worth about $143 million.

In practice, that means a low-lying property can take water even when the sky above it is clear, because the river rises upstream. Check the FEMA flood map for your address, and ask your insurer about separate flood coverage before the next storm, not after it.

The plan describes a 1950s flood that put water across Main Street past West and Mill Streets, and it notes that in 2005 a flood damaged a business in town. In 1955 the French River dams failed upstream, including a breach in Charlton, and Southbridge felt it even though no Southbridge dam failed.

What a High Hazard dam rating does and does not mean

Westville Lake Dam, run by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers just outside downtown, is rated High Hazard in the plan. The rating is easy to misread.

What it means

If the dam failed, the consequences downstream would be serious. High Hazard describes those consequences, not how likely a failure is.

What it does not mean

It is not a forecast of failure, and it does not say your street will flood. The plan notes an Emergency Action Plan revised in 2020.

What you can do

Check your address on the FEMA flood map, and ask your insurer whether you carry flood coverage.

Insurance and flood: what Massachusetts says

Flood is the most common surprise in a claim. The Massachusetts Division of Insurance states that standard homeowners policies do not cover flood damage, including flooding away from oceans and rivers.

QuestionWhat the state guidance says
Is flood covered by standard homeowners?No. Standard homeowners and commercial policies do not cover flood damage.
What about a sump pump failure or water backup?Some coverage may be available by a rider (endorsement). Ask your agent.
How long is the NFIP wait?Policies usually have a 30-day waiting period, with some exceptions for loan-related purchases.
Who can buy NFIP coverage?Renters, homeowners and business owners. Southbridge participates in the program.
What should I do after a flood?Report the claim right away, photograph inside and outside including water height, and keep damaged and undamaged items apart.

Check your policy or ask your insurer. Nothing here promises a claim will be approved.

How a flood job usually runs

The order matters more than the speed of any one step.

  1. Safety checkPower, gas and structure are confirmed safe. Protective gear goes on.
  2. Water outPumps and extraction units remove standing water. The first steps may overlap with emergency water removal.
  3. Material outSoaked carpet, pad, insulation and low drywall are cut out and bagged.
  4. Clean and sanitizeSurfaces are cleaned, treated and deodorized to Category 3 precautions.
  5. DryWater mitigation equipment, including air movers and dehumidifiers, runs until readings reach dry standards.
