Water Damage Restoration in Globe Village, Southbridge, MA

Globe Village is the older, denser side of Southbridge, where two-family houses and brick worker housing sit close together near Main Street and the Quinebaug River. If water is already inside, call (508) 446-9244 and describe what you see.

Where Globe Village sits and why the buildings matter

The Massachusetts Historical Commission's Southbridge town report places Globe Village on the north bank of the Quinebaug River, stretching along Main Street. It describes worker housing on Main Street, brick worker housing off Pleasant and Gilmore Streets, duplexes on Union and Cross Streets, and middle-income houses on High and Sayles Streets. No official boundary is published, so treat those streets as landmarks, not a border.

That mix matters for water. In a duplex or three-decker, a failed supply line upstairs usually soaks the ceiling and walls of the unit below, and the household downstairs may not know until a stain appears. A job there can involve more than one household and a landlord, so decisions about drying and repair need to be coordinated early.

What water does inside a multi-unit building

These are the patterns a restoration crew checks first when a building holds more than one apartment.

  • Ceiling stain below a bathroom: the leak is often one floor above and has been running longer than the stain suggests.
  • Wet plaster and lath: where walls are older plaster instead of drywall, water can travel through the cavity and the wet line on the surface understates the damage.
  • Basement seepage: the lowest unit or the shared basement takes the water from every floor, and stored belongings go first.
  • Shared drain stacks: a blockage in a shared line can push dirty water out of the lowest fixture, which makes it a contaminated-water job.
  • Radiator and boiler piping: older heating loops can leak slowly inside a wall, so a steady damp spot near a radiator deserves a moisture reading.

The river, the brook and a 1950s flood

The town's 2024 draft hazard mitigation plan says the Quinebaug River runs through downtown and that a historic 1950s flood put water across Main Street, past West Street and Mill Street. The National Register lists the Hamilton Woolen Company Historic District as roughly bounded by McKinstry Brook, the Quinebaug River and Mill Street.

The plan does not say which Globe Village blocks were wet, so this page claims nothing about any single street. For your own address, check the FEMA flood map and ask the Southbridge building department.

Everyday water is far more common than river water. The same plan describes undersized and outdated stormwater infrastructure that causes localized flooding even in smaller storms, and a sanitary sewer in generally poor condition with heavy inflow in wet weather. A basement floor drain that backs up during a downpour is a sewage problem, so read about sewage backup cleanup before touching anything.

Safety checklist before anyone starts cleaning

"Stay out of any room where water is near outlets, cords or the panel, and call the electric utility or 911 for a wet panel or any smell of gas.",

"Turn off the water at the main valve or the fixture valve, but only if you can reach it while standing on dry floor.",

"Leave standing water to a pump. A household vacuum is not rated for it and can shock you.",

In a shared building, tell the other units and the landlord right away. A leak that started upstairs can be live in more than one apartment.

What a restoration visit usually involves

The published reference for this work is ANSI/IICRC S500, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it. In practice a visit follows this order.

  1. Find and confirm the sourceThe crew checks that the water is stopped, then maps where it went with a moisture meter and, where useful, a thermal camera.
  2. Extract standing waterPumps and extraction units remove what is on the floor first, because every hour of standing water spreads moisture into framing.
  3. Remove what cannot be driedSoaked insulation, saturated drywall and swollen trim come out so the cavity can dry. Contaminated materials are bagged and removed.
  4. Dry and monitorAir movers and dehumidifiers run until readings reach the dry standard. See structural drying for how that is measured.
  5. Record the workPhotos, moisture logs and a written scope give you and your insurer a clear record of what was found and done.

Documenting damage for an insurance claim

Photograph the damage before moving anything, and photograph again after the water is out. Moisture readings and an itemized scope of proposed or completed work can help describe the loss to your insurer.

Coverage depends on the cause of the damage and on your policy. Sudden pipe failures are treated differently from slow leaks, and standard homeowners policies do not cover flood. Tenants should check renters coverage and tell the landlord in writing. Ask your insurer what applies before assuming anything.
