What Is Mildew? A Southbridge, MA Homeowner's Guide

Mildew is a type of mold. The word usually describes a flat, powdery or fuzzy growth on a surface, often white or gray, that has found a little moisture to live on.

By the Southbridge Water Damage Pros team · 2026

Call (508) 446-9244

Is mildew different from mold?

People use the two words as if they were opposites, and the difference is mostly about habit. Mildew is the name most people give to light, surface-level growth on things like a shower curtain or a window sill. Mold is the broader word, and it often describes thicker, darker growth that has gone into a material such as drywall, wood or carpet.

So a gray film on the bathroom caulk and a green patch inside a basement wall may both be mold, in different amounts. The useful question is not what to call it. It is how much there is, what it is growing on and where the water is coming from.

Mildew and mold at a glance

These are rough, everyday patterns rather than rules. Only an assessment can tell you what is on a particular surface.

FeatureWhat people call mildewWhat people call mold
LookFlat, powdery or fuzzy, often white or grayFuzzy, slimy or spotty, in many colors including black, green and brown
Where it sitsOn the surface of tile, grout, caulk, paint and fabricOn the surface and often into porous materials like drywall and wood
Typical spotShower, window sill, damp towel, closet shelfWet wall cavity, carpet pad, ceiling after a leak, flooded basement
CleanupOften handled with detergent and a scrub brush for a small spotMay need removal of the affected material, depending on size and material
What it meansA moisture or humidity problem somewhere nearbyThe same, usually on a bigger scale

Both need moisture. Remove the moisture and growth has nothing to feed on. The EPA puts it this way: the key to mold control is moisture control.

Where mildew shows up in a house

Mildew follows moisture, so start looking where water collects or air stays damp. Crawl spaces are another damp place, and crawl space water damage covers them.

The shower and bathroom

Grout lines, caulk at the tub and the underside of a shower curtain stay wet after every use. A fan that vents outdoors helps, and one that blows into the attic does not.

Window sills and frames

Winter condensation runs down cold glass and pools on the sill. Wiping it away matters more than the cleaner you pick.

Cardboard in a basement

Boxes on a concrete floor draw moisture up from the slab. The bottom inch turns soft and speckled long before the top does.

Closets on exterior walls

Cold outside walls and clothing packed tight leave air still. Look behind shoes and along the baseboard.

Under sinks

A slow drip or a weeping trap keeps the cabinet floor damp. Check the base of the cabinet with a flashlight.

What to do about a small spot

For a small patch on a hard, nonporous surface like tile, glass or a sealed countertop, a simple routine is often enough. Stop and call a professional if the growth is large, keeps returning or sits on drywall or carpet.

  1. Find the moistureRun a hand along the nearby wall and feel for dampness. Look for a dripping line, a missing fan or a spot where condensation collects.
  2. Protect yourselfOpen a window, wear gloves and keep children and pets out of the room. Do not mix cleaning products.
  3. Clean the surfaceScrub with detergent and water, then rinse and dry. Cleaning removes growth from hard surfaces. It does not repair a leak and it does not clean out porous material like carpet or drywall.
  4. Dry completelyUse a towel, a fan or a dehumidifier. Surfaces left damp invite the problem back.
  5. Watch the spotIf it returns within weeks, treat it as a sign of an unfixed water source and look further.

Signs that mildew is really a moisture problem

A single spot behind a shower curtain is a housekeeping job. These are different.

  • It keeps coming back: Repeated growth in the same place means the source is still active.
  • The surface is soft or stained: Drywall that feels spongy or paint that bubbles points to water inside the wall.
  • A musty smell without a visible patch: Moisture may be trapped behind finishes, under flooring or in a wall cavity.
  • It follows a leak or flood: Mold after a plumbing failure or storm calls for mold remediation after the water is dealt with.
  • It covers a large area: Several square feet or more usually calls for a professional look, because cleaning is not the same as remediation.

Health questions about exposure are for a physician. We cannot tell you what a particular growth means for anyone's health.

What Southbridge's vacancy rate means for mildew

The town's 2024 draft hazard mitigation plan lists vacancy at about 7.8 percent of housing units. That figure comes from older Census data, so it is a guide rather than a current count. It still points to a real situation: a share of the housing stock sits empty at any given time.

A vacant or unheated unit is a classic place for mildew to start. With no one running a fan, opening a window or noticing a drip, humidity builds and a small leak can run for weeks. Winter makes it worse, because cold walls collect condensation and an unheated bathroom stays damp.

If you own, manage or are about to buy a vacant property, check it on a schedule. Look under sinks, at the basement floor and along exterior walls for dampness or a musty smell. Where water has already been in, water damage cleanup comes before any mildew cleaning.
