How to Choose a Mold Removal Service in Southbridge, MA

A good mold removal service fixes the moisture source, contains the work area, removes affected porous materials and proves the result with independent testing.

By the Southbridge Water Damage Pros team · 2026

Call (508) 446-9244

Three quotes can describe three different jobs

Call three companies about the same basement wall and you may hear three plans. One offers to spray and paint. One wants to cut out four feet of drywall. One talks about sealing the room with plastic and running filtered air machines for several days.

Those are not the same job at different prices. Only one of them addresses what mold actually needs, which is moisture, and what the standard asks for. The ANSI/IICRC S520 standard for mold remediation describes how professionals assess, contain and clean affected areas. Ask any company whether its crews work to it. A careful answer is a good sign.

What a proper mold removal job includes

Details vary by building and by how far the mold spread, but the sequence below is what to expect from a legitimate mold remediation job.

  1. Find and fix the waterMold needs moisture. A leaking supply line, a roof leak or a condensate line backing up has to be repaired first, or the work will be repeated.
  2. Assess the extentMoisture meters and a thermal imaging camera map where materials are wet, including behind finishes the eye cannot see.
  3. Contain the areaPlastic sheeting, a sealed doorway and negative air pressure keep disturbed spores from spreading into clean rooms.
  4. Filter the airHEPA air scrubbers run during the work, and HEPA vacuums clean surfaces afterward.
  5. Remove porous materialsMoldy drywall, insulation, carpet and ceiling tile are cut out, bagged and removed. Wood framing is cleaned, sanded or treated as the situation requires.
  6. Dry the structureAir movers and dehumidifiers bring readings to normal, which is the same drying principle behind water mitigation.
  7. Verify the resultAn independent party performs post-remediation clearance testing, so the company that did the work is not grading itself.

Red flags to walk away from

Any one of these is reason enough to keep looking.

A promise that mold will never return

No honest company can say that. Mold returns when moisture does.

Bleach as the whole plan

Surface cleaning can remove staining on hard surfaces. It does not fix the leak, and it does not clean mold out of drywall or carpet.

No containment

Scrubbing or cutting without sealing the area can push spores into the rest of the building.

Testing by the same company

Ask who performs clearance. Independent testing keeps the result honest.

A price over the phone for an unseen room

Commercial space and older masonry buildings

Businesses face the same sequence with extra constraints: tenants, ventilation systems and neighboring units. A rented suite with a roof leak above it may need the landlord to repair the source before the tenant's contractor can finish. Ask the company how they will keep the HVAC from spreading disturbed material to other suites.

Large brick and masonry buildings add their own problems, such as thick walls that hold moisture, long plumbing runs and basements that stay damp. Mold removal there is often tied to water damage cleanup first, because the damp has to be dealt with before cleaning means much.

What Southbridge's mill-era buildings mean for mold work

Southbridge was shaped by the American Optical Company, which closed in 1984 and had more than 6,000 employees at its height. Before that, the Quinebaug River powered sawmills, gristmills and later textile mills, and mill-era buildings remain in town. This is general history and says nothing about the condition of any particular building.

The practical lesson is about size. Large old industrial and commercial buildings often have big roofs, long pipe runs and basements or lower levels next to a river. A leak can run unnoticed in a space nobody visits, so mold work on a building like that begins with a careful moisture survey, not a guess.

If you manage or lease space in an older building, ask any mold removal company how it will map moisture across the whole floor, how it will contain one section and who will perform clearance testing. Those three answers tell you more than the quoted total.
