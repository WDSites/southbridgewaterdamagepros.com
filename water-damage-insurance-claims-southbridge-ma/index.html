Water Damage Insurance Claims: What to Document in Southbridge, MA

Most claim trouble starts with missing records, not with the water. Photograph first, measure the water, report promptly and keep every receipt.

By the Southbridge Water Damage Pros team · 2026

Call (508) 446-9244

Start with the records an adjuster will ask for

Ten minutes of documentation can save weeks of back-and-forth. An adjuster was not there when the pipe burst or the basement filled, so your photos, notes and receipts are the only version of events they can check.

Nothing here guarantees a result. Whether a loss is covered depends on the cause and the wording of your policy. Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, gradual leaks and neglect often are not, and flood usually needs separate flood coverage. Check your policy or ask your insurer before you assume either way.

The claim checklist, in order

Work through these as soon as it is safe. If anything involves electricity, gas or sewage, handle safety first.

  • Report promptly: Call your insurer as soon as you can and ask what they require, including how they want photos and receipts submitted. Policies commonly set notice conditions, so check yours.
  • Photograph everything: Capture each affected room wide, then close up. Include the outside of the house, the source of the water and any visible stains or sagging.
  • Record the water height: Photograph a tape measure against the wall or a stair riser where water stood, and write down the number and date.
  • Separate damaged from undamaged: Move wet items to one area and undamaged items to another. Do not throw anything away until it has been photographed and listed.
  • Build a home inventory: List each item with a description, approximate age and replacement cost. A walk-through video narrated room by room helps.
  • Keep every receipt: Hotel nights, equipment, supplies and emergency repairs may all matter to your claim.
  • Mitigate further damage: Stop the source, remove standing water and start drying. Policies commonly expect you to prevent additional loss, so see water mitigation for what that involves.

Keep a simple log of calls: who you spoke with, the date and what was said. A page of notes beats memory three weeks later.

How a typical claim moves

Insurers differ, but the path usually looks like this. After a storm, the storm water damage page covers what is different about documenting wind and rain damage.

  1. NoticeYou report the loss and receive a claim number. Write it on everything.
  2. MitigationThe structure is dried and ruined materials are removed. Photograph before anything is thrown out.
  3. Adjuster visitAn adjuster inspects the damage and reviews your photos, inventory and invoices.
  4. Scope and estimateThe repair scope is written and compared with your policy. Ask questions if a line item seems missing.
  5. RepairOnce drying readings are normal, water damage cleanup and rebuild work can begin.

Do not hold up safety for paperwork

If the panel is wet, the smell of gas is present or sewage has backed up, leave the area and call the utility or 911 before photographing anything. A claim can be documented later. Contaminated water is a flood damage restoration job that needs protective gear, so do not wade in to take pictures.

What the state's documentation advice means for you

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance gives specific advice for flood claims. Report the claim immediately. Photograph the outside and inside of the home, including the water height. Keep damaged and undamaged items separate, and use a home inventory. That is the same list above, which is why it is worth following for any water loss.

The state page does not speak to claim deadlines or to sudden versus gradual damage, so those are matters for your own policy. Treat the state advice as a habit rather than a rule on timing: the sooner you document, the less you depend on memory.

In practice that means taking pictures before the first fan runs. In Dudley or Webster, where the French River runs along the town line, a home that took on groundwater should get a tape-measure photo on the wall before anyone starts pulling drywall.
