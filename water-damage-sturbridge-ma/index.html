Slopes, ponds and 23 dams

Slopes of 15 to 25 percent cover about 14.3 percent of Sturbridge, according to the town hazard plan. Rain runs downhill fast, and culverts that cannot take the load back up onto roads and into foundations. That is why the same handful of spots flood again and again.

The plan counts 23 dams in town, 1 rated High Hazard and 8 rated Significant Hazard. Walker Pond, Cedar Pond, Old Mill Pond, Ovide Pond, Upper Pond, Fish Pond and Gibb's Brook all have dams. A hazard rating describes what a failure would cause, not how likely it is. For you it means groundwater runs high near these waters after a wet week, so a damp basement is a reason to call before the moisture climbs the drywall.

The plan also lists 504 parcels in the 100-year floodplain, 123 of them with structures worth about $40.6 million. It records 5 NFIP claims totaling $31,297 and 2 repetitive-loss residences. Standard homeowners policies usually exclude flood, so check the FEMA flood map for your address and ask your insurer about flood coverage. Before we arrive

Four steps that help most in a Sturbridge house, whether it is on town water or a private well.

Find the valve

On town water, the main shutoff is where the line enters the house. On a private well, shut off the pump breaker as well as the tank valve, or the pump keeps pushing water.

Check the panel

If the basement is wet and the electrical panel is down there, do not wade in. Call the utility or 911 about the hazard first.

Lift what you can

Move rugs, boxes and electronics off the floor. Leave heavy furniture alone, since wet carpet and slippery steps cause injuries.

Keep people and pets out

If the water came from a sewer line near Big Alum Lake or Cedar Lake, treat it as contaminated and keep children and animals away.

Town water, sewer or well: who to call about what

Which system serves your home changes the first phone call, and it is worth knowing before the emergency.

  • Town water: The Sturbridge Water Department serves most commercial and suburban residential areas, so a street-side break is a utility call and an inside break is ours.
  • Sewer: Sewer extends around Big Alum Lake and Cedar Lake. A backup in those neighborhoods is Category 3 water, so stay out and call.
  • Private wells: Other homes use private wells, which means a flooded well house or failed pump is your water problem to solve.
  • Permits and health questions: Ask the Building Inspector's Office about rebuild permits and the Board of Health about sewage or well concerns.
