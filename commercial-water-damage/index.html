Commercial Water Damage in Southbridge, MA

Commercial water damage is water inside a building used for business, where the loss includes the structure, the contents and the days the doors stay closed. Licensed and insured restoration professionals handle assessment, extraction and drying.

Call the 24/7 line, shut off the water supply if you can reach the valve, and move inventory and papers off wet floors. If electrical or gas is a risk, call the utility or 911 first.

What a restoration crew can and cannot do for a business

A restoration crew deals with the water and what it touched. That means finding where it went, pumping it out, drying the structure and protecting what can be saved. It does not mean fixing the pipe, rewiring a panel or rebuilding a storefront, which are separate trades.

Saying that up front matters, because a building owner has to line up several people. Restoration is one of them, and it works best when the leak has been stopped by a plumber or the owner.

Older mixed-use buildings near Main Street are covered on the Downtown Southbridge page, and the area around the former optical campus is covered under Lensdale.

Old brick, industrial floors and the Southbridge building stock

The Quinebaug River powered sawmills, gristmills and later textile mills in Southbridge, and mill-era buildings remain in town. General history also records that the American Optical Company closed in 1984 after employing more than 6,000 people at its height. Thick masonry walls and heavy timber floors hold water longer than modern framing, so drying a building like that takes patience and measurement.

The town's 2024 draft hazard mitigation plan says manufacturing is the largest single-block employment sector and that many industrial uses sit in the floodplain. If your building is in one of those areas, check the FEMA flood map and read the flood section of your policy now, before water is on the floor.

Safety checklist for the first hour in a flooded commercial space

Get people out of any area where water is near live electrical equipment, and do not stand in water to reach a panel. Cut power only to dry, reachable circuits and call the utility or 911 for any electrical or gas risk before the restoration line.

Wet floors are a slip hazard for staff and visitors, so block the area and post a sign. If the water came from a toilet or a drain, treat it as contaminated and keep everyone out. See sewage backup cleanup for how that is handled.

What a crew does on a commercial water loss

The scope depends on the building, but the core work follows the same path.

  • Assessment: Moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras map where water traveled through floors, walls and ceilings.
  • Extraction: Truck-mounted and portable extraction units remove standing water. See emergency water removal.
  • Structural drying: Air movers and LGR or desiccant dehumidifiers run, with readings taken on visits until the structure is dry. See structural drying.
  • Contents protection: Items are lifted, covered or moved so they are not sitting in water or in the drying zone.
  • Cleaning and deodorizing: Affected surfaces are cleaned and treated, with contaminated materials removed first.
  • Documentation: Photos, moisture readings and equipment logs are kept so the owner has a record for an insurance claim.

Work follows the published ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard as a reference. Ask any company whether its crews work to it.

How a commercial job is usually sequenced

Business owners usually ask how to keep the doors open while this happens. The order below gives the crew room to work.

Insurance for a business owner: check your policy first

Sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe or a failed supply line, is often covered under a commercial property policy. Gradual leaks and neglected maintenance often are not. The Massachusetts Division of Insurance states that standard commercial business policies do not cover flood damage, so flood needs a separate policy.

FEMA reports that about 25 percent of businesses do not reopen after disasters and urges owners to check that coverage is enough. Take dated photos before cleanup, keep receipts and log what was moved. Nothing here promises a claim will be approved, so ask your insurer what it needs.

Mistakes that stretch out a commercial loss

A few habits raise the damage and complicate the claim.

  • Waiting for a full inventory before calling: Photograph quickly, then call. Drying should start while you list.
  • Throwing items out before photographing them: Your insurer may want to see what was lost.
  • Running heat and closing the doors to dry it: Without dehumidification, moist air stays in the building.
  • Skipping the plumber: If the source is not fixed, the water returns.
  • Letting wet drywall and ceiling tile sit: EPA guidance says water-damaged areas should dry within 24 to 48 hours to avoid mold growth.
